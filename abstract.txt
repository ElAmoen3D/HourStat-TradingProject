This study investigates the statistical validity of the &quot;Hour Stat,&quot; a short-term price action
pattern observed in intraday futures markets, as applied to the E-mini Nasdaq-100 (NQ)
futures contract using one-minute OHLCV candlestick data spanning approximately two
years of continuous trading history (September 2023 through September 2025). The Hour
Stat proposes that when the opening price of a given clock hour (H2) falls within the high-
low range established by the immediately preceding hour (H1), and price subsequently
sweeps beyond H1&#39;s range within the first twenty minutes of H2, a mean-reversion back
through H2&#39;s opening price constitutes a high-probability, tradeable outcome.
To systematically evaluate this hypothesis, a Python-based backtesting framework was
developed utilizing the pandas library for time-series data manipulation. The algorithm
iterates over every consecutive hour pair in the dataset, applies the three-stage pattern filter
(range containment, directional sweep, return confirmation), and records the outcome of
each qualifying setup. Results are disaggregated by sweep direction (bullish vs. bearish),
hour of day, and calendar month to identify temporal clustering or structural biases in
pattern performance.


Across the full dataset, the algorithm identified 6,954 qualifying setups, of which 5,257
resolved successfully, yielding an aggregate win rate of 75.6%. Bullish (Up) and bearish
(Down) setups performed comparably at 76.3% and 74.8% respectively, suggesting the
pattern&#39;s mean-reversion logic is directionally agnostic. Win rates were stable across all
three calendar years sampled — 74.9% (2023), 74.2% (2024), and 77.9% (2025) —
indicating the pattern is not an artifact of a single market regime. Intraday performance,
however, showed notable dispersion: the 9:00 hour produced the highest resolution rate at
92.7% across 314 setups, coinciding with the U.S. equity open session. Monthly variation
was modest, with win rates ranging from 72.3% (November) to 79.1% (May), offering no
strong evidence of seasonal structural breaks. These findings are broadly consistent with the
preliminary observations from the most recent 100 setups, which yielded a comparable win
rate exceeding 75% and showed no directional asymmetry.


Taken together, these results offer robust preliminary support for the Hour Stat as a
statistically non-random phenomenon in NQ futures, with a win rate that holds consistently
above 74% across multiple years, directions, and market sessions. The most actionable
finding is the pronounced hour-of-day effect, particularly the outperformance during the
New York open, which warrants further investigation as a session-filtered trading strategy.
Critical limitations remain: the analysis does not yet account for risk-adjusted returns, bid-
ask spread, slippage, or stop-loss placement, all of which are necessary to evaluate the
pattern&#39;s practical profitability. Future work will incorporate these execution-realistic
parameters, apply formal binomial significance testing against a chance baseline, and
extend the dataset further back toward 2009 to stress-test the pattern&#39;s consistency across a
wider range of volatility regimes.
